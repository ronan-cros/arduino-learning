### How does a LED work ?

## What I learnt: 
1. What is a semi-conductor ? A semi-conductor is a material that be changed to an insulator and a conductor by heating it or cooling it down.
2. Structure of a semi-conductor: The semi-conductor contains atoms organised in a crystal lattice, composed of a conducting band and a valance band with a gap in between.
3. How does a semi-conductor work ? When the semi-conductor is cooled down, the electrons are all in the valence band while the conducting band has no electrons. When a potential difference is applied in the circuit, there is no current flowing in either band. When the semi-conductor is heated up, electrons gain energy and shift to the conducting band. The semi-conductor is able to conduct current in both bands as electrons can flow freely for both of them.
4. Different types of semi-conductors: Semi-conductors can contain impure atoms between the bands. N-type semi-conductors will have negatively charged impure atoms close to the conducting band to transfer electrons to that band, allowing current to only flow in it. P-type semi-conductors will contain positively charged impure electrons close to the valence band and will steal electrons from it, allowing electrons in the band to move freely because of the space.
5. When the n-type and the p-type semi-conductor are combined in a circuit, the electrons and positive charge won't be able to flow in both materials. If a potential difference is applied in the circuit, the electrons from the n-type's conductor band will be able to flow to the p-type's conductor band, and the positive charges from the p-type's valence band will be able to flow to the n-type's valence band. Now, the electrons from the conductor band will be able to shift to the valence band and will emit photons in the process, emitting light from the LED. 
6. On Diodes: When the potential difference is reversed, the electrons cannot flow anymore (the barrier between both types is lifted too high). This is how a diode works, current can only flow in one direction, this is why the cathode and anode of the LED has to be coherent with the direction of current. The cathode corresponding to the n-type semi-conductor part of the led has to be towards the negative part of the voltage, and the anode corresponding to the p-type semi-conductor has to be towards the positive voltage. 
7. Resistors: As the potential difference in the circuit increases, the current increases exponantially. If current is too high, the LED will burn out and the smoke inside of it will be let out, not being able to work anymore. A resistor is added to the circuit to regulate the current passing in the LED. 
8. Colour of the LED: The gap between the semi-conductor's bands determine the colour of the LED, this is called the energy gap.
